💡 First Principles Review
  1. Long-Term Fundamentals: Company fundamentals have a direct effect on share prices. This principle applies only over a long period of time.
  2. Cash Flows vs. Noise: The long-run performance of stocks is largely based on the expected future cash flows of the companies attached to them—it has very little to do with what the stock did over the past week or month.
📌 Summary: Pat Dorsey's 5 Rules for Successful Stock Investing
1. Do Your Homework: Understand the business model, unit economics, and competitive dynamics thoroughly before buying.
2. Find Economic Moats: Invest in companies protected by sustainable competitive advantages (brand, switching costs, network effect, cost moat).
3. Have a Margin of Safety: Only buy stocks trading at a significant discount to their estimated intrinsic value.
4. Hold for the Long Haul: Let compounding work. Frequent trading incurs taxes, transaction fees, and behavioral mistakes.
5. Know When to Sell: Sell when you made an analytical mistake, fundamentals permanently deteriorate, or better opportunities arise—not due to market panic.
